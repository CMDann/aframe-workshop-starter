## What you may do

**Use these assets in a scene you build during, or after, a PIZZA.EXE / Spatial Storytelling workshop.** That includes:

- Placing the models in your own A-Frame scene
- Publishing that scene to a public URL — Glitch, GitHub Pages, or anywhere else
- Sharing the link with anyone
- Keeping your scene online indefinitely after the session ends
- Modifying how the assets appear *in your scene* — scale, position, materials, lighting

**Run the workshop.** Facilitators may fork this repository and use the assets to teach a session, including one they are paid to run.

## What you may not do

- **Redistribute the assets on their own** — as a download, an asset pack, a marketplace listing, or a library, whether free or paid
- **Use them in another product**, game, application, or commercial work that is not a workshop scene
- **Create derivative models** from them for use outside a workshop scene
- **Train machine-learning models** on them
- **Remove or obscure attribution**, or claim authorship

## The short version

Build whatever you like with these in a workshop scene and publish it proudly. Don't lift the assets out and ship them somewhere else.

## Two things worth being clear about

**This repository is public, and public GitHub repositories can be forked.** GitHub's Terms of Service grant every user the right to view and fork any public repository, and that grant overrides anything written here for as long as the repository stays public. Making the repo private is the only way to prevent forking. The restrictions above still govern what someone may lawfully *do* with the assets once forked — but they do not, and cannot, prevent the copy itself.

**Attribution, not permission, is the ask for workshop scenes.** A participant publishing their scene does not need to write for permission. A credit is appreciated but not required.
